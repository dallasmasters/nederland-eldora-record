Resolution 2026-31, with my proposed amendments

Dallas Masters, Nederland resident · Amendments Nov. 7, 2026 · Original draft Oct. 18, 2026; revised Oct. 27, 2026

The Board will consider Resolution 2026-31 at its Nov. 9, 2026, meeting. The Town Attorney drafted it as a revision of the resolution I submitted in October. Below is the Town’s text with the amendments I’m asking the Board to adopt: deadlines it can extend by a vote (a term sheet by Jan. 31, a signed agreement and financing by March 31, and a closing by June 30, 2027), a $3 million ceiling on total costs, an appropriation before any further spending, the budget items required, formal loans if Water or Sewer cash is used, a dated final accounting, and a review before any vote on new deal documents. I recommend adopting it with these amendments. The Town Attorney hasn’t reviewed them. My reasons are on my opinion page.

TOWN OF NEDERLAND

Boulder County, Colorado

RESOLUTION 2026-31

A RESOLUTION LIMITING FURTHER ELDORA ACQUISITION SPENDING, REQUIRING A FINAL ACCOUNTING AND BOARD APPROVAL OF RELATED ENGAGEMENTS, AND PROVIDING FOR CONTINUED UPDATES ON THE PROPOSED ACQUISITION OF ELDORA MOUNTAIN RESORT

WHEREAS, the Board of Trustees has pursued the potential acquisition of Eldora Mountain Resort ("Resort") and, by Resolutions 2025-03, 2025-11, 2025-16, 2025-19, and 2025-22, authorized or ratified the engagement of legal counsel, underwriters, financial advisors, and other consultants for that purpose without stating a dollar limit; and

WHEREAS, by Resolution 2025-17, the Board declared its intent to reimburse acquisition costs from the proceeds of future revenue bonds; and

WHEREAS, as of the Oct. 20, 2026, warrant list, the Town had paid $1,195,949 in acquisition costs, charged to the General Fund; and

WHEREAS, the Purchase and Sale Agreement with POWDR expired, as its closing deadline of April 30, 2026, passed without a closing; the authority to sell bonds under Ordinance 885, Section 5(c), lapsed on July 19, 2026; and the Town has stated that it continues to pursue the acquisition, that discussions with POWDR remain active, and that it expects to negotiate updated agreements if the financing proceeds as anticipated; and

WHEREAS, in response to feedback from prospective bondholders, the Town has been restructuring the transaction to provide stronger security tied to the asset, including a sale-leaseback structure, to reduce the overall amount of debt, to model interest rates and poor snow years more conservatively using the most recent season’s actual results, to separate discretionary capital improvements from the order of payments under the bond documents, and to explore philanthropic and other outside funding for those improvements; and

WHEREAS, the acquisition costs paid to date have been recorded as recoverable from bond proceeds and the Town has stated that, once a transaction closes, those costs are reimbursable from bond proceeds, but no bonds have been issued, the authority to issue them has lapsed, and their repayment depends on agreements and a bond ordinance that do not yet exist; and

WHEREAS, Ordinance 873 established the Mountain Recreation Fund as an enterprise to be kept separate from the Town’s General Fund and other funds, but that fund is not among the funds appropriated in the 2026 budget, and the acquisition costs have been charged to the General Fund; and

WHEREAS, the Town’s Sept. 30, 2026, updated estimates show that the Town has incurred about $2.76 million in transaction-related costs that would not be reimbursed if the transaction does not close; and

WHEREAS, the 2026 budget does not include appropriations for these costs, and C.R.S.§ 29-1-110 provides that no officer or employee may spend or contract to spend in excess of the amounts appropriated; and

WHEREAS, the Board is preparing the 2027 budget and finds that limiting further spending, firm milestones, and regular public reporting will protect the General Fund while the Board continues its work to finalize the transaction;

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE BOARD OF TRUSTEES OF THE TOWN OF NEDERLAND, COLORADO:

Section 1. Definition. “Acquisition costs” means fees and expenses for legal, financial, advisory, due-diligence, transition, and other services related to the potential acquisition, financing, or operation of Eldora Mountain Resort that the Town would owe whether or not a closing occurs. It does not include amounts payable only from bond proceeds at a closing.

Section 2. Spending halt; limited work toward a term sheet. The Board continues to pursue the acquisition and recognizes that the restructured transaction, including a sale-leaseback structure, reduced debt, stronger security for bondholders, updated financial modeling, possible philanthropic and other outside funding, and continued discussions with POWDR, requires additional work before a term sheet can be presented to the Board. Accordingly, the Town shall incur no acquisition costs for work performed after the effective date of this resolution, except (a) work the Town Manager requests under Section 4, and (b) work by firms already engaged for the acquisition that the Town Manager, in consultation with the Town Attorney, determines is reasonably necessary to develop a term sheet for presentation to the Board, not to exceed $150,000 in total without further Board authorization by resolution. No amount under clause (b) shall be incurred until the Board adopts a supplemental appropriation for it under C.R.S. § 29-1-109. In addition, if a report under Section 4 or Section 8 shows total acquisition costs, paid and owed, above $3,000,000, the Town shall incur no further acquisition costs until the Board approves continuing by resolution. 1Any such work shall be within amounts appropriated, consistent with C.R.S.§ 29-1-110, and reported to the Board under Section 8. The term sheet is not a public document and shall be presented to the Board in executive session to the extent permitted by law. Within three business days, the Town Manager shall direct each firm engaged for the acquisition, in writing, to stop billable work for the Town other than the work described in this section. This section does not affect amounts the Town already owes for work performed before the effective date.

Section 3. Board approval of engagements. No new engagement, contract amendment, or expansion of scope that would incur acquisition costs may be made without prior approval of the Board by resolution. To the extent Resolutions 2025-03, 2025-11, 2025-16, 2025-19, and 2025-22 authorize the Town Manager to engage counsel, advisors, or “additional consultants, as necessary,” that authority is limited by this section.

Section 4. Final accounting. The Town Manager shall ask each firm engaged for the acquisition for (a) fees paid, billed, and unbilled for work performed through October 16, 2026, and through the effective date of this resolution2, and (b) any amount the Town would owe under the engagement’s terms if the acquisition does not close. At the next regular meeting after that periodDecember 1, 2026, regular meeting, the Town Manager shall present a written report, included in the meeting packet, itemizing those amounts by firm and comparing the total with the Town’s Sept. 30, 2026, estimate of approximately $2,500,000 and with the updated estimates of about $2.76 million cited above, explaining any difference3.

Section 5. Milestones. 4The Board of Trustees recognizes the community’s desire for clear milestones and a reliable timeline for the acquisition. The Board is committed to transparency and will identify anticipated milestones and timing as information becomes sufficiently developed to provide meaningful and reliable estimates. The following milestones apply: (1) by January 31, 2027, a term sheet presented to the Board; (2) by March 31, 2027, a purchase agreement approved by the Board and signed by all parties, and written financing commitments sufficient to close; and (3) by June 30, 2027, a closing. If a milestone is not met and the Board has not extended it by resolution adopted before that date, the Town’s pursuit of the acquisition shall end; the Town Manager shall then direct all firms to stop work other than work needed to wind down, and shall present a final accounting at the next regular meeting. The Board will update the community as those milestones are established or changeeach milestone is met or extended.

Section 6. Diligence review. The Board has already incurred costs for independent financial and operational review and due diligence relating to the proposed acquisition5, including analyses prepared by SE Group and Causey Public Finance, LLC. The proposed bond financing has also been reviewed in connection with the underwriting and marketing of the bonds by the Town’s underwriters, RBC Capital Markets and BofA Securities, Inc. The Board intends to continue its financial and operational due diligence as the proposed acquisition progresses, including consideration of whether projected Resort revenues are sufficient to support the acquisition and operation of the Resort and pay debt service under a range of operating conditions. Before any Board vote on a new purchase agreement, bond ordinance, lease, or financing agreement for the acquisition, the Board shall receive a written review from a qualified advisor whose compensation does not depend on a closing, addressing whether the Resort’s revenue can pay the debt at then-current interest rates and through low-snow seasons, and any obligation the General Fund or other Town funds would bear.6

Section 7. Town Funds. The Town’s acquisition and operation of the Resort would be undertaken through the Mountain Recreation Enterprise. As an enterprise, the Mountain Recreation Enterprise must comply with applicable Colorado law, including TABOR. In addition, the bond Indenture will require that debt service on the bonds be paid from revenues generated by the Resort, rather than from the Town’s General Fund or other Town funds. If acquisition costs are paid, directly or through pooled cash, with money belonging to the Water Fund or the Sewer Fund, the amount shall be recorded as an interfund loan to the General Fund, approved by Board resolution stating the amount, interest rate, repayment schedule, and source of repayment, and a finding that the lending fund can provide it without hampering its operations, capital plans, or loan covenants. Before any such loan, the Town Attorney shall advise the Board in writing whether a loan repaid over more than one fiscal year requires voter approval under Colo. Const. art. X, § 20(4)(b), and whether the Water and Sewer Funds’ loan agreements with the Colorado Water Resources and Power Development Authority limit it.7

Section 8. Public reporting. At the first regular meeting of each month, the Town Manager shall provide a written report, included in the meeting packet, showing acquisition costs paid, billed but unpaid, and still owed, by firm; any further spending the Board has authorized under Section 2; total acquisition costs compared with the Town’s Sept. 30, 2026, estimate of about $2,500,000 and the updated estimates of about $2.76 million3; and 8the status of each milestone in Section 5; and each fund’s share of the Town’s pooled cash and investments, and the amounts owed between funds, as of the most recent month-end.

Section 9. Budget. The Town intends to comply with this section through the budget adoption process. The Town Manager shall present to the Board any or all9all of the following:

1. A transfer, supplemental appropriation, or revised appropriation under C.R.S. § 29-1-109 for acquisition costs incurred by December 31, 2026, shown as a separate line item;

2. A separate line item in the proposed 2027 budget for acquisition costs expected to be incurred in 2027; and

3. With the proposed 2027 budget, a plan for the General Fund if the acquisition does not close, including how unrecovered acquisition costs would be funded.

Section 10. Accounting and budget process. The Board has engaged an accounting firm, not engaged in the acquisition negotiations, that is assisting with the budget process and accounting functions for the Town. The firm has a professional obligation to account for these expenses in accordance with Governmental Accounting Standards Board (GASB) requirements and statutory municipal budgeting requirements. Because that work already covers the matters raised by the resident, no separate report is required. Instead, the Town Manager shall include the following information in the budget materials presented to the Board in a public meeting packet, so that the Board and the public can see how each matter is answered:

1. How acquisition costs have been recorded, and whether they remain recoverable. The budget materials shall show how acquisition costs are recorded in the Town’s general ledger, including in the account titled “Eldora - reimbursable expenses,” and the accounting treatment, under GAAP/GASB, of amounts the Town expects to recover from bond proceeds at closing and of amounts it would not recover if no closing occurs;

2. Which fund bears the costs. The budget materials shall identify the fund to which acquisition costs have been charged, explain why they were charged to the General Fund rather than the Mountain Recreation Fund established by Ordinance 873, and present any transfer, interfund balance, or appropriation proposed to correct or document that treatment, as described in Section 9;

3. Each fund’s share of pooled cash and amounts owed between funds. The budget materials shall present, from the Town’s general ledger as of the most recent month-end, each fund’s share of the Town’s pooled cash and investments and the amounts owed between funds, including any amounts owed to the General Fund for acquisition costs; and

4. Whether appropriations were required. The budget materials shall state whether acquisition costs incurred and/or paid in 2025 and 2026 required appropriations under C.R.S. § 29-1-110 and, if so, the supplemental or revised appropriation under C.R.S. § 29-1-109 proposed to address them.

The cost of this accounting work is not subject to Section 2.

Section 11. Effect. Except as provided in Section 5, n4Nothing in this resolution requires the Town to end negotiations or terminate any agreement. Where this resolution conflicts with a prior resolution, this resolution controls. This resolution takes effect on adoption.

RESOLVED, APPROVED, AND ADOPTED this 9th day of November, 2026.

Why each amendment

  1. A ceiling on spending. The resolution’s recitals say the 2026 budget has no appropriation for these costs, so the $150,000 would first need one under C.R.S. § 29-1-109. The $3 million trigger caps the whole cost of trying: paid plus owed is about $2.87 million by the firms’ October confirmations, and firms that haven’t confirmed could add to it, so passing $3 million calls for a public vote rather than stopping work outright.
  2. A date for the final accounting. As drafted, Section 4 asks only for amounts through Oct. 16, 2026, which the firms already confirmed in October, and reports "at the next regular meeting after that period," a period the draft no longer defines. This sets the Dec. 1 meeting, before the 2027 budget is adopted, and covers work through adoption.
  3. One set of figures. The recitals cite "updated estimates" of about $2.76 million, while Sections 4 and 8 compare with about $2,500,000. The reports should compare with both and explain the difference.
  4. Deadlines the Board can extend. Dates the Board can extend by a vote before each one passes. A deadline then forces a public decision without ending a deal that is close. March 31 and June 30, 2027, leave the first half of 2027 for a signed agreement and a closing after the 2026–27 ski season.
  5. A missing word. The draft reads "relating to the proposed,"; "acquisition" appears to be missing.
  6. A review before any vote on deal documents. Section 6 describes past diligence, including review by the underwriters, whose pay a 2025 Board memo anticipated would depend on a financing. This adds a written review from an advisor not paid on closing before the Board votes on new deal documents.
  7. Formal loans if utility cash is used. The 2025 audit reports the General Fund’s cash as negative, covered by pooled cash that is mostly the Water and Sewer Funds’. The Board approved its 2014–2016 utility loans to the Downtown Development Authority by resolution with an interest rate, a deadline, and a finding that the lending fund could spare the money (Res. 2014-33, 2015-06, 2016-20). Whether a multi-year loan between the Town’s own funds needs voter approval isn’t settled in the records.
  8. Pooled cash every month. Section 10(3) asks for each fund’s share of the pooled cash once, in the budget materials. Monthly reporting would show whether utility cash is being used as the costs are paid.
  9. The budget items required. As drafted, the Town Manager presents "any or all" of the three items, so the plan for the General Fund if the acquisition does not close could be left out.

Separately, for the 2027 budget

These belong in the budget work rather than the resolution. I've asked the Board to take them up in its FY2027 budget discussion, which begins Nov. 9:

  1. Record acquisition costs as General Fund spending in the year incurred, including amounts owed at year-end, and record any reimbursement from bond proceeds when received.
  2. Explain the new “Eldora Expense” account on the Nov. 9 warrant list, whose year-to-date balance is shown as $2,188,218.53 (the record).
  3. Appropriate the 2025 and 2026 acquisition costs at a public hearing before Dec. 31, 2026, and show the costs as a separate line item in the 2027 budget.
  4. Present a plan for the General Fund if the acquisition doesn’t close: how about $2.9 million would be covered, and over how many years.
  5. If Water or Sewer cash is needed, include the loan terms, with a finding that the utilities can provide it given their fire-flow project and state loan commitments.
  6. Ask the auditor selected for the 2026 audit to address how the acquisition costs, the receivable, and any amounts owed between funds are recorded.

My original draft (October 2026)

This is the resolution I'm suggesting the Board of Trustees adopt at its Nov. 9, 2026, meeting. Because the sale is unlikely to close, it would halt further Eldora acquisition spending, require a final accounting of what the Town owes, and require a Board vote for any new engagement or further spending. It keeps firm deadlines: if the deal misses them, the effort ends. It also requires an independent review before any vote on new deal documents, monthly public reports, and budget planning for the costs. Suggested values are filled in for the Board to accept or change. The Town Attorney has not reviewed it. My reasons are on my opinion page; the facts in the recitals come from Town records, linked below.

TOWN OF NEDERLAND, COLORADO

RESOLUTION NO. 2026-[number]

A RESOLUTION HALTING FURTHER ELDORA ACQUISITION SPENDING, REQUIRING A FINAL ACCOUNTING AND BOARD APPROVAL OF RELATED ENGAGEMENTS, AND ESTABLISHING MILESTONES FOR THE PROPOSED ACQUISITION OF ELDORA MOUNTAIN RESORT

WHEREAS, the Board of Trustees has pursued the potential acquisition of Eldora Mountain Resort and, by Resolutions 2025-03, 2025-11, 2025-16, 2025-19, and 2025-22, authorized or ratified the engagement of legal counsel, underwriters, financial advisors, and other consultants for that purpose without stating a dollar limit; and

WHEREAS, by Resolution 2025-17, the Board declared its intent to reimburse acquisition costs from the proceeds of future revenue bonds; and

WHEREAS, as of the Oct. 20, 2026, warrant list, the Town had paid $1,195,949 in acquisition costs, charged to the General Fund; and

WHEREAS, the closing deadline in the Purchase and Sale Agreement, April 30, 2026, passed without a closing; the authority to sell bonds under Ordinance 885, Section 5(c), lapsed on July 19, 2026; and the Town has stated that it continues to pursue a restructured transaction; and

WHEREAS, the acquisition costs paid to date have been recorded as recoverable from bond proceeds, but no bonds have been issued, the authority to issue them has lapsed, and their repayment now depends on agreements and a bond ordinance that do not yet exist; and

WHEREAS, Ordinance 873 established the Mountain Recreation Fund as an enterprise to be kept separate from the Town's General Fund and other funds, but that fund is not among the funds appropriated in the 2026 budget, and the acquisition costs have been charged to the General Fund; and

WHEREAS, the Town's Sept. 30, 2026, update estimates that the Town would owe about $2.5 million in transaction-related costs if the sale does not close; and

WHEREAS, the 2026 budget does not include appropriations for these costs, and C.R.S. 29-1-110 provides that no officer or employee may spend or contract to spend in excess of the amounts appropriated; and

WHEREAS, the Board is preparing the 2027 budget and finds that halting further spending, firm milestones, and regular public reporting will protect the General Fund while any further negotiations proceed;

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE BOARD OF TRUSTEES OF THE TOWN OF NEDERLAND, COLORADO:

Section 1. Definition. "Acquisition costs" means fees and expenses for legal, financial, advisory, due-diligence, transition, and other services related to the potential acquisition, financing, or operation of Eldora Mountain Resort that the Town would owe whether or not a closing occurs. It does not include amounts payable only from bond proceeds at a closing.

Section 2. Spending halt. The Town shall incur no acquisition costs for work performed after the effective date of this resolution, except for work the Town Manager requests under Section 4, unless the Board authorizes them by a further resolution. Within three business days, the Town Manager shall direct each firm engaged for the acquisition, in writing, to stop billable work for the Town other than that requested work. This section does not affect amounts the Town already owes for work performed before the effective date.

Section 3. Board approval of engagements. No new engagement, contract amendment, or expansion of scope that would incur acquisition costs may be made without prior approval of the Board by resolution. To the extent Resolutions 2025-03, 2025-11, 2025-16, 2025-19, and 2025-22 authorize the Town Manager to engage counsel, advisors, or "additional consultants, as necessary," that authority is limited by this section.

Section 4. Final accounting. Within 14 days, the Town Manager shall ask each firm engaged for the acquisition for (a) fees paid, billed, and unbilled for work performed through the effective date of this resolution, and (b) any amount the Town would owe under the engagement's terms if the acquisition does not close. The Town Attorney shall identify which of the Town's obligations are owed now and which would be owed only at a closing. At the next regular meeting after that period, the Town Manager shall present a written report, included in the meeting packet, itemizing those amounts by firm and comparing the total with the Town's Sept. 30, 2026, estimate of about $2,500,000.

Section 5. Milestones. The following milestones apply:

  1. By Jan. 31, 2027, a purchase agreement approved by the Board and signed by all parties, and written financing commitments sufficient to close.
  2. By April 30, 2027, a closing.

If a milestone is not met and the Board has not extended it by resolution adopted before that date, the Town's pursuit of the acquisition shall end. The Town Manager shall then direct all firms to stop work other than work needed to wind down, shall incur no further acquisition costs, and shall present a final accounting at the next regular meeting.

Section 6. Independent review. Before any Board vote on a new purchase agreement, bond ordinance, lease, or financing agreement for the acquisition, the Board shall receive a written review from a qualified advisor whose compensation does not depend on a closing. The review shall address whether the resort's revenue can pay the debt at then-current interest rates and through low-snow seasons, and any obligation the General Fund or other Town funds would bear.

Section 7. Public reporting. At the first regular meeting of each month, the Town Manager shall provide a written report, included in the meeting packet, showing acquisition costs paid, billed but unpaid, and still owed, by firm; any further spending the Board has authorized under Section 2; total acquisition costs compared with the Town's Sept. 30, 2026, estimate of about $2,500,000; and the status of each milestone in Section 5.

Section 8. Budget. The Town Manager shall present to the Board:

  1. a transfer, supplemental appropriation, or revised appropriation under C.R.S. 29-1-109 covering acquisition costs paid or owed in 2026, shown as a separate line item;
  2. a separate line item in the proposed 2027 budget for acquisition costs still to be paid; and
  3. with the proposed 2027 budget, a plan for the General Fund if the acquisition does not close, including how unrecovered acquisition costs would be paid.

Section 9. Accounting review. The Town Manager shall propose, for Board approval by resolution, an independent certified public accounting firm, not otherwise engaged for the acquisition, to perform an agreed-upon procedures review. The firm shall report its findings to the Board in writing, included in a meeting packet, within 45 days of the effective date of this resolution. The review shall address:

  1. how acquisition costs have been recorded, including in the account titled "Eldora - reimbursable expenses," and whether they remain recoverable;
  2. why acquisition costs were charged to the General Fund rather than the Mountain Recreation Fund established by Ordinance 873;
  3. each fund's share of the Town's pooled cash and investments, and amounts owed between funds, as of the most recent month-end; and
  4. whether acquisition costs paid in 2025 and 2026 required appropriations under C.R.S. 29-1-110.

With the report under Section 4, the Town Manager shall present each fund's share of the pooled cash and investments, and amounts owed between funds, from the Town's general ledger as of the most recent month-end. The cost of this review is not subject to Section 2.

Section 10. Effect. Except as provided in Section 5, nothing in this resolution requires the Town to end negotiations or terminate any agreement. Where this resolution conflicts with a prior resolution, this resolution controls. This resolution takes effect on adoption.

ADOPTED on [date], 2026.

Halting spending doesn't reduce what the Town already owes; it keeps the total from growing. "Acquisition costs" covers fees owed to outside firms, not Town staff time.
