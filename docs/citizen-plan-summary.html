Citizen Plan for Nederland's Budget Crisis

One-page summary. Written by Nederland residents Melody Baumhover, Karen Blakemore, Dallas Masters, and Ben White, from the Town's public records. Nov. 24, 2026. Not a Town document. The full plan gives the reasons and records behind each step.

The problem

The Town has paid or owes about $2.9 million for work on the possible Eldora purchase, and $1.62 million of it is still owed to seven firms. Staff forecast the General Fund at −$2.18 million at the end of 2026 and −$2.53 million at the end of 2027. State law requires the 2027 budget to balance. Staff propose about $2.6 million in transfers from the Water, Sewer, and Community Center funds, with no reduction in the General Fund's yearly operating spending.

The plan

  1. Stop new spending on the acquisition, and hold the bills. No new Town spending on the acquisition. Hold every acquisition bill until the Board approves a settlement in public. The plan doesn't decide whether the Town buys Eldora.
  2. No change to the 2026 budget. No supplemental appropriation, amendment, or transfer. 2026 spending is forecast at $180,221 under the approved budget.
  3. Charge each fund its own costs.
  4. Settle the bills for less, through counsel, with each settlement approved in public.
  5. Reduce the Sheriff contract to a sergeant and two deputies, and ask the County to bill the Town for only two of those three positions, because deputies also cover the area outside town. Keep the co-responder and the traffic shifts.
  6. Borrow from the Community Center Fund first, using only money voters didn't restrict. Operations stay funded, and capital work is deferred.
  7. Cut General Fund spending, after a review of the fund's spending and an organizational review, keep the 3% raise for employees, and ask the Downtown Development Authority to take on the Visitor Center.
  8. Borrow from Sewer and Water last. At the end of 2027, a loan of up to $400,000 from Sewer, then a loan from Water for the amount still needed.

What the plan achieves

  • The Community Center, Sewer, and Water funds' reserves are rebuilt: about $1.75 million is borrowed from them and repaid. The Town's proposal transfers $2.6 million from the same funds permanently, with no repayment.
  • Spending falls about $667,000 a year, so revenue exceeds spending by $40,000 to $90,000 each year through 2031. Under the Town's proposal the General Fund falls to about −$2.1 million again.

What the numbers assume

The settlement savings are a planning figure, not an offer. The Sheriff savings assume the County bills the Town for two of the three positions, and pays at least half of the co-responder, as it did for 2026. The money from the Community Center, Sewer, and Water funds is lent, not transferred. The Board approves each loan by resolution, and each loan is made at the end of 2027, only for the amount still needed. Water's money is mostly sales tax voters dedicated to water debt, so it can be lent but not transferred. Sewer's and the Community Center's money is fee money, so if a loan can't be counted toward the budget, it could be a transfer that is returned. If the Town's auditor won't count a Water loan toward the budget, the Board has to find about $523,000 elsewhere.
