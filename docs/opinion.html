My opinions on the Eldora purchase

Dallas Masters, Nederland resident · Oct. 17, 2026

Everything else on this site is drawn from Town records and official sources and stands on its own. This page is my opinion. The facts it relies on are linked to the Eldora Record, where you can check them and reach your own conclusions.

  • The Town Board did not have bad intentions, but it has put the Town at significant and unprecedented financial risk.
  • The idea of owning Eldora is attractive, but it might still be a daunting undertaking and risky even if the deal finalizes.
  • We got here by what looks like groupthink about the attractiveness of the idea and promises of long-term financial gain, without much questioning of the financial costs should the deal not go through. This is evidenced by lots of discussion about the costs being covered by the eventual bond proceeds but very little about what happens if the deal falls through, and about the increasing probability that it won't happen.
  • The probability that the deal goes through is low and getting lower; my current rough estimate is a 15% probability that it goes forward. This is due to:
    • financing getting harder because of rising interest rates, with prospective bondholders already turning down the first deal's terms (where things stand)
    • staffing issues: the town manager who shepherded the deal left, an interim town manager has taken over, the treasurer position is open, and the current work is "primarily being performed by the Mayor" without paid consultants (Oct. 20, 2026, memo)
    • nothing keeping POWDR from selling to another buyer (the purchase agreement has expired)
    • many instruments needing to be redone: a new purchase agreement, a new or amended bond ordinance, and new bond documents
  • So it is increasingly looking like, if the deal fails, the Town will end 2026 with the General Fund at about −$1.9 million, a very large deficit for a town whose General Fund spends about $3.2 million a year, against an already bleak financial forecast (the Town's own 2026 budget deficit). This will likely result in borrowing from other funds and some combination of cutting non-essential services and staff, selling Town assets, and having little to no discretionary funding for many years.
  • With such a low probability of the deal closing, the Town Board should either end the effort now or continue only with clear limits:
    • a firm deadline: a signed purchase agreement and committed financing by Jan. 31, 2027, and closing by April 30, 2027, one year after the original closing deadline
    • a cap on any further spending the Town would owe even if the deal fails
    • an independent review of the final terms before any vote, testing whether the resort can carry the debt at current interest rates and through bad snow years without help from the Town
    • regular public accounting of the costs and of progress on the deal
    Either way, it should plan the 2027 budget for the deal failing and begin the process of dealing with the financial repercussions.
