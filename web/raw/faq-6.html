Eldora FAQ 6.0

Eldora FAQs Posted on October 05, 2026

THE RESPONSES BY THE TOWN PROVIDED BELOW ARE FOR INFORMATIONAL PURPOSES ONLY AND ARE SUBJECT TO CHANGE AT ANYTIME.


The Town is currently operating under non-disclosure agreements with various transaction participants related to the potential acquisition of Eldora. In addition, the Board of Trustees has approved a bond ordinance authorizing the issuance of Enterprise revenue bonds, which brings specific legal requirements governing public communications while the bond process is underway. This means the Town must avoid speculation and discuss only what has been formally decided, in order to prevent misunderstanding and avoid creating expectations that may not be supported by final financing documents.


Because of these legal constraints, the Town documents and addresses all questions through written responses following appropriate review via these FAQs.


This approach is intended to ensure that information is provided consistently and accurately and in compliance with applicable legal requirements. It does not reflect a change in the substance of the Town’s work or decision-making.


We appreciate the community’s engagement and patience and will continue to share information through the Town’s public forums as it becomes appropriate to do so.


I. Transaction Basics & Governance


1. Why did the Board choose straight up municipal ownership instead of a coop, special district, or a private buyer?


Local control was the big driver. Owning Eldora Mountain Resort outright means Nederland can align programming to Nederland values, pursue grant money private owners can’t touch, and keep every dollar of excess profit working for the community. Having the Town Enterprise own the mountain keeps things simple, clear, and directly accountable.


2. Will the public get to vote?


No general Town funds or taxpayer dollars are being used to purchase Eldora Mountain Resort. The purchase is being made through the Mountain Recreation Enterprise Fund, which operates like a self-sustaining business. It’s funded by revenue from the ski area - like lift tickets, rentals, and lessons - not by taxes. Under Colorado’s TABOR law, because this fund qualifies as an “enterprise,” it’s allowed to issue revenue bonds without a public vote.


This structure also acts as a firewall: it keeps business operations like the ski area financially separate from the Town’s general fund, which is where your tax dollars go. While it’s possible that grants or other non-tax funding sources might support the effort, no general Town resources will be used.


And while a ballot measure isn’t required, every major step still goes through a public Board of Trustees vote, in open meetings where community members are always welcome to weigh in.


3. Will Eldora stay on the Ikon Pass?


Yes, the plan is to keep Eldora on the Ikon Pass. We're excited to continue that partnership.


4. Will my property taxes or utility bills change because of this purchase?


No. The bond repayment comes solely from Eldora Mountain Resort revenue, not from local taxes or fees.


5. Does the deal include land for future terrain or workforce housing?


The only currently approved terrain expansion by the US Forest Service is the Jolly Jug expansion. Regarding workforce housing, the assets scheduled to be purchased include Nederhaus, which is currently used for employee housing.


Following the acquisition, the Town expects to engage in broader planning to evaluate capital improvement needs and long-term priorities across the mountain.


6. What is the Town’s role with 303 Ski regarding Eldora?


Following the contemplated acquisition, the Town expects to rely on Eldora’s existing management and staff for day-to-day operations. The Town may evaluate potential collaboration with 303 Ski or other consultants on limited initiatives, subject to completion of the acquisition, further review, and evaluation under the purchasing policy. The Town does not currently anticipate 303 Ski serving in a primary strategic or operational role.


7. [NEW] Why did you part ways with 303 Ski?


What is the Town’s role with 303 Ski regarding Eldora? Following the proposed acquisition, the Town intends to rely on Eldora’s existing management and on-site staff for the day-to-day operation of the ski area. The intent is for Eldora to continue functioning under its current operational framework, with day-to-day decisions handled by the team currently running the mountain.


The Town made this determination for several reasons. First, the on-Mountain team is already operating the Resort with many folks in the leadership ranks with 10, 15, or more than 20 years experience at the Resort. The new General Manager for the Resort, Andrew Gast, has expertise in running a non-profit ski resort. In addition, through the two-year period set forth in the Transition Services Agreement with POWDR, the Town believes it will have the appropriate combination of operational continuity and technical expertise during the transition period.


Second, in evaluating the Town’s objective—including the goal of fostering an independent, community-aligned mountain—the Town determined that direct ownership, combined with input from multiple qualified advisors as needed, rather than reliance on a single outside group, may provide greater flexibility and access to specialized experts.


Finally, incorporating 303 Ski into the operational structure would have added an additional administrative layer between the Town and the mountain management team, which the Town concluded could increase complexity and cost. The Town concluded that direct integration between Town Manager Cain, the Town’s CEO, with Andrew, the CEO of the Resort would support clear communication and accountability between the Town and resort leadership. Community input and feedback from on-mountain staff were considered as part of this decision. The Town’s goal is to implement processes and an organizational structure that serve both the operational needs of the resort and the broader community.


There has been public discussion and some confusion about the role of 303 Ski in this process. To clarify, there was no agreement for 303 Ski to serve as investors in the acquisition, and they were never participants in the transaction as equity partners or financial backers.


Some comments have suggested that 303 Ski “walked away” because the deal was unfavorable. The Town is not aware of any evaluation by 303 Ski of the financial terms of the transaction. The Town made the decision not to move forward with 303 Ski in a role related to the acquisition. In addition, 303 Ski was not provided access to the Town’s financial modeling or transaction details and therefore did not evaluate the financial aspects of the transaction.


After the acquisition is complete, the Town may consider whether there are limited opportunities to work with 303 Ski or other consultants on specific, discrete initiatives. Any such consideration would be subject to further review, consistency with the Town’s purchasing policies, and approval through established public processes.

The Town’s role as owner is expected to be a long-term stewardship. Professional on-mountain leadership is expected to remain responsible for lift operations, snowmaking, guest services, and daily resort functions.

11. [NEW] How will you retain continuity since the BOT is always fluctuating?


Nederland operates under a council-manager form of government. The Board sets policy and long-term direction, and the Town Manager is responsible for administration and operational oversight.


If the Town becomes the owner of Eldora Mountain Resort, day-to-day mountain operations would be overseen by the on-mountain management. The General Manager of the Resort would report through the Town Manager, consistent with the enterprise structure and existing municipal governance framework.


This structure establishes a clear line of responsibility: the Board establishes policy; the Town Manager provides executive oversight, and professional mountain leadership manages operations.


In addition, the enterprise structure itself supports continuity. Financing agreements, operating frameworks, and multi-year planning processes establish obligations and operational guardrails that extend beyond individual election cycles.


Boards may change over time. Operational consistency is supported by the professional management structure, established reporting relationships, and the enterprise framework.


12. [NEW] Why was there an NDA signed?


The Town has entered into non-disclosure agreements with transaction participants to obtain and evaluate confidential information necessary for due diligence and responsible evaluation of the proposed transaction.


These agreements do not replace or limit public meetings, notices, or Board actions required for approvals. Rather, they restrict disclosure of specific confidential business, operational, or financial information while negotiations and financing are underway.


Non-disclosure agreements are a common component of transactions involving private parties because they allow the parties to share sensitive information needed for evaluation while protecting proprietary and contractual interests.

II. Finance and Bonds


16. What is the purchase price?


On January 6, 2026, the Board of Trustees approved Ordinance 882, which allows the execution of an Asset Purchase Agreement to buy Eldora Mountain Resort. The purchase price starts at $120 million. This amount is subject to downward adjustment as set forth in the Asset Purchase Agreement.


17. How is the acquisition of Eldora Mountain Resort being paid for?


The acquisition is being paid for by issuing bonds through the Town’s Mountain Recreation Enterprise. The Board of Trustees approved Ordinance 885, which authorizes the issuance of these bonds. The bonds will be paid back only from revenue generated by operating Eldora Mountain Resort. They are not paid for with property taxes, sales taxes, or the Town’s general fund, and they do not create debt for the Town itself.


18. Can locals or private investors help fund the acquisition?


Under applicable laws, the bonds will be offered only to a limited group of qualified institutional and accredited investors. They are not available to the general public. However, parties who believe they may qualify and are interested in investing can contact Town Manager Jonathan Cain for more information about the process.


19. [NEW] How are you ensuring this is a fiscally sustainable acquisition?


Financial modeling is one of several tools the Town is using to evaluate potential scenarios, risks, and constraints associated with the acquisition. This work is intended to inform decision-making rather than predict or guarantee outcomes.


As part of this process, the Town is working with experienced external professionals. RBC Capital Markets and Bank of America are serving as underwriters, and Stifel is serving as the Town’s financial advisor. These firms are assisting the Town by providing analysis, reviewing assumptions, and helping evaluate options consistent with their respective roles.


The modeling and related analysis are ongoing, and certain information cannot be finalized or shared until key steps in the process are complete. When information can be responsibly communicated, it will be shared through appropriate public channels.


Financial considerations are being evaluated alongside operational, legal, and community factors using established municipal processes and professional input. Community questions and feedback are an important part of that broader discussion.


20. [NEW] Is the acquisition undercapitalized?


This question is one of the reasons the Town is undertaking detailed financial modeling and working with professional advisors. The purpose of this work is to test assumptions, understand constraints, and identify potential risks or funding gaps under a range of scenarios.


That analysis is ongoing, and it would not be responsible to characterize conclusions before it is complete and presented through the proper formal disclosure process. What we can commit to is that concerns about capitalization are part of the review and are not being ignored or dismissed.


The Town’s due diligence materials reference potential future capital expansion concepts across the mountain, including items such as a mountain-top restaurant, lift infrastructure such as the Corona lift, and a possible Jolly Jug expansion. These concepts are planning-level only and do not represent approved projects, timelines, or funding commitments.


As the process moves forward and information can be shared appropriately, the community will have the opportunity to review and discuss it through the Town’s public processes.


21. What if the resort has a poor snow year? Does the Town need to cover that loss?


No. The Town’s general fund is not required to cover operating losses if Eldora Mountain Resort has a poor snow year. Under the Indenture of Trust, the resort’s revenues are managed within the Mountain Recreation Enterprise and used first to pay operating costs and required reserves. The Indenture requires reserve and stabilization funds that are specifically designed to help manage weaker seasons and year-to-year revenue changes.


The bonds are payable only from revenues generated by Eldora Mountain Resort. They are not backed by the Town’s general fund or taxes, and the Indenture is structured to reduce financial risk and avoid the need for Town financial support, even in challenging operating years.


22. What does the aggregate principal amount of the bonds mean?


The bond ordinance sets a maximum total amount for these bonds. Specifically, the aggregate principal amount cannot exceed $225 million.


This means the Town’s Mountain Recreation Enterprise is authorized to issue bonds up to $225 million in total, but it is not required to issue that full amount. The actual amount issued will depend on the final financing needs and market conditions. In addition to funding the acquisition price, bond proceeds must also be used to fund required reserves, capital improvements, and costs of issuance.


23. Will any “junk bonds” be issued?


The current plan contemplates the issuance of municipal revenue bonds payable solely from the revenues of Eldora Mountain Resort. The bonds would not constitute a general obligation of the Town and would not be backed by the Town’s taxing power.


The bonds are expected to be issued without a credit rating. “Junk bonds” (more commonly known as high-yield bonds) are non-investment grade debt securities with credit ratings below BBB- (S&P/Fitch) or Baa3 (Moody's), offering higher interest rates to investors to compensate for increased default risk. This financing is unique for the municipal bond market, and without an operating history, the Town is not able to obtain an investment-grade credit rating. The Town’s Enterprise will have the ability to refinance its debt prior to maturity, and it may be possible to pursue a credit rating at that time.


Municipal securities are not automatically rated. Obtaining a rating requires a separate application process, the provision of detailed financial and operating information, and the payment of fees to rating agencies. The Mountain Recreation Enterprise is a new entity and therefore does not currently have a credit rating.


The absence of a credit rating does not, by itself, determine the risk profile or market acceptance of a bond. Interest rates and pricing are influenced by multiple factors, including market conditions at the time of sale, the structure and security of the bonds, investor demand, and the financial performance of the pledged revenue source.


It is not uncommon, particularly for smaller municipalities or infrequent issuers to issue unrated municipal revenue bonds. Prospective investors must review the offering documents carefully and make their own assessment of the risks and merits of the bonds. At the time of purchase, investors will be required to execute an “Investor Letter” which codifies certain representations of the investors’ ability to diligence their own risk assessment.


24. [NEW] What are the financial aspects? Are these municipal tax-free bonds? What is the interest rate?


The final financial terms, including interest rates and other pricing details, will be determined through the bond marketing and closing process and will be disclosed to the public after the acquisition. The acquisition is anticipated to close on April 30th, 2026.


Because the bonds are being issued through a limited offering, detailed offering materials are not expected to be broadly distributed. The Town will share financial information through its public processes as appropriate and consistent with legal requirements.


The bonds are expected to be structured to qualify for tax-exempt treatment where permissible under federal tax law; however, the final tax status of any portion of the bonds will depend on the final structure and applicable legal requirements.


25. Is the Eldora resort profitable – does it make money?


As part of the acquisition process, the Town has conducted extensive financial due diligence, including review of historical operating performance and detailed forward-looking financial modeling. That analysis has been supported by experienced municipal finance professionals and ski industry specialists.


The proposed financing structure is based on the mountain’s projected ability to support itself through its own revenues. The intent is to structure the enterprise so that operations and debt service are supported by resort performance rather than general tax dollars.


It can be frustrating not having every detail of Eldora’s revenues yet. But it’s worth asking: would the Town have gotten this far - working with experienced municipal finance professionals, running detailed financial modeling, and pursuing a structure that requires the resort’s own revenues to cover its debt obligations - if the numbers didn’t support the resort’s ability to repay, cover infrastructure improvements, maintain the existing infrastructure, and put enough away for a few bad snow years?


No business is without risk, and no guarantees are being made. The Board’s decision is grounded in professional analysis, industry expertise, and conservative financial planning.


26. [NEW] Will special districts like the library and fire department lose revenue because of the acquisition?


We’ve heard concerns about whether special districts could lose property tax revenue if certain areas become Town-owned as part of the acquisition, and we want to clarify how that works.


Special districts are funded, in part, through property taxes that are assessed on specific parcels within their boundaries, based on ownership and valuation.


Property owned by a municipality is generally exempt from property taxation under Colorado law. As a result, if parcels become Town-owned, those parcels may no longer be subject to property taxes.


Property taxes in Colorado are paid in arrears, meaning the taxes paid in 2026 are based on property ownership and valuation in 2025. As a result, any change in ownership related to the acquisition would not affect taxes already assessed for the current cycle.


The Town is working directly with the County Assessor to identify which parcels, if any, would become Town-owned following the acquisition and how those parcels are currently assessed. This review will help clarify timing and any potential fiscal effects.


The Town recognizes the importance of special district services and will continue coordinating with affected districts as information becomes available.


As information is confirmed through the assessor’s process, it will be shared through the Town’s public channels.


27. [NEW] Are there any financial covenants related to or a trigger for expansion in any of the financing documents?


No financing covenants are expected to require the Town to undertake expansion projects.


Like most revenue bond financings, the financing documents are expected to include customary financial and operational covenants related to matters such as maintaining the enterprise, meeting debt service obligations, and conditions for issuing additional debt. These provisions govern financial performance and stewardship but do not mandate specific capital improvements.


Any future capital projects would be considered through the Town’s budgeting, planning, and public decision-making processes.


28. [NEW] Is there operational and financial clarity on the model?


Detailed financial modeling is part of the Town’s due diligence and decision-making process. Because the Town is operating under confidentiality obligations and the financing process is ongoing, the full financial model cannot be shared at this time.


While complete revenue and cost information cannot be publicly released at this stage, the Town has worked with experienced municipal finance professionals to evaluate scenarios, risks, infrastructure needs, and operational sustainability.


The financing structure is designed so that resort revenues are intended to support operating expenses, capital maintenance, capital improvements, and debt service obligations. This structure is evaluated through modeling that considers variable conditions, including weather variability, capital repair cycles, and potential growth.


Additional financial information will be shared through appropriate public channels after the acquisition, when it can be released consistent with legal and financing requirements. The acquisition is anticipated to close on April 30th, 2026.

No public-private partnership structure has been approved as part of the acquisition. The current structure is an enterprise model in which the resort revenues are intended to support operations and bond repayment, separate from the Town’s general fund. This structure is designed to isolate enterprise finances from general municipal revenues, therefore also insulating taxpayers, subject to the terms of the financing documents and applicable law.


Any future operational or financing arrangements would be considered through the Town’s public decision-making processes.

Alignment with the Town’s sustainability goals is a policy determination made by the Board of Trustees, based on adopted community priorities related to environmental stewardship, recreation, transportation impacts, and long-term community resilience. This policy alignment is separate from financial feasibility.


To evaluate financial and operational considerations, the Town has relied on multiple independent professionals with expertise in municipal finance and mountain resort operations:


RBC Capital Markets and Bank of America serve as underwriters and assist with structuring and marketing the municipal revenue bonds.


Stifel is serving as the Town’s financial advisor, providing independent financial analysis, reviewing assumptions, and advising on financing structure and risks.


SE Group, a mountain resort planning and recreation economics firm, conducted an operational and market assessment to evaluate resort operations, infrastructure needs, visitation trends, and long-term viability considerations.


In addition, Town staff and legal counsel have reviewed operational, financial, legal, and governance considerations as part of due diligence.


The Town has used financial modeling and professional analysis to evaluate potential scenarios, risks, capital needs, and revenue considerations. This work is intended to inform decision-making rather than predict outcomes.


31. [NEW] How big are the underwriting and legal fees? Are they capped?


Final underwriting and legal costs are determined at closing and therefore are not finalized at this stage, although estimates are included in the Town’s financial modeling.


These costs are typical transaction expenses and are expected to be paid from bond proceeds rather than the Town’s general fund.


While the fees are not subject to a single fixed cap, they are governed by negotiated agreements, customary market practices, and the scope of services provided.

Because the transaction is expected to close after the ski season, the number of employees working at that time is expected to be lower than peak seasonal staffing levels.

Employment remains subject to standard Town hiring requirements and applicable law.

We’re still figuring that out. For employees who are hired, the Town, through the Mountain Recreation Enterprise, will use reasonable efforts to offer pay, benefits, and accrued paid time off that are substantially similar to those provided prior to closing.

The Town’s Mountain Recreation Enterprise looks forward to working with Eldora employees and is focused on completing the acquisition and supporting a smooth transition after closing. At this time, no decisions have been made regarding union-related matters.


Under Colorado and federal law, municipalities operate under a different labor framework than private employers, and towns are not required to recognize or bargain with unions in the same way. That legal framework would apply to Eldora following the acquisition.


After closing, the Town intends to take time to better understand Eldora’s operations, staffing needs, and workplace practices. Any future approach to employee relations would be considered thoughtfully, consistent with applicable law and the Town’s responsibilities as a public employer. No commitments or determinations have been made regarding union recognition or collective bargaining.


35. What happens to seasonal J-1 visa staff?


Right now, we don’t plan to change how Eldora hires seasonal J-1 workers.


36. [NEW] In a worst-case scenario where the Mountain Recreation Enterprise can’t cover payroll, does the Town’s General Fund step in? What’s IS the worst-case scenario?


No. As we mentioned earlier, the resort pays its own way. Its operations and payroll are funded only from the revenues it generates as an enterprise. The Town’s General Fund, which supports services like streets, utilities, and community programs, cannot be used for resort expenses. Operating expenses — including payroll — are paid from enterprise revenues and available enterprise funds.


If the Enterprise were unable to make a scheduled debt service payment, the financing documents provide for the use of debt service reserve funds. If reserves were drawn down, bondholders’ remedies would be limited to those set forth in the bond documents. The bonds are secured by enterprise revenues, not by the Town’s General Fund.


In a revenue shortfall, the Town would need to evaluate operational adjustments, cost reductions, or other measures to stabilize the enterprise. Ownership of the resort would remain with the Town; however, the financial impacts would depend on the specific circumstances at that time.


37. Does the federal 40-hour cap for overtime apply? If there is a 40-hour limit, what is the plan to keep departments functioning and ensuring employees' hours and pay are adequate?


No. The federal 40-hour overtime rule and state wage and hour laws do not automatically apply to the Town’s Mountain Recreation Enterprise in the same way they apply to private employers. As a local government enterprise under TABOR, the Town’s Mountain Recreation Enterprise has the flexibility to manage work hours in a way that reflects the seasonal nature of Eldora Resort’s operations.


No final decisions have been made about overtime or scheduling after the transition. The Town understands that some departments rely on longer hours during peak periods and does not intend to disrupt effective operations or reduce employee pay through arbitrary limits. Any future policies will follow applicable laws and be shared clearly with employees and the community.


IV · Liability & Risk Management


38. Why not set up a separate limited liability company to shield liability, and what kind of insurance will the Town’s Mountain Recreation Enterprise carry?


Under Colorado law, towns like ours are not allowed to create or own private companies such as an LLC.


The Town plans to operate Eldora Mountain Resort as an “enterprise,” which is a legal structure allowed under Colorado law, including provisions of TABOR, and commonly used for public operations like utilities and recreation facilities. This approach provides clear public oversight and accountability while allowing the resort to be managed separately from the Town’s general operations.


The Town is also working to put appropriate insurance coverage in place for Eldora Mountain Resort. Coverage details are still being finalized and will depend on market conditions and final operating decisions. The goal is to carry insurance that is appropriate for a ski resort and a public enterprise.


39. How is the Town addressing legal and liability concerns related to operating a ski resort?


The Town has evaluated the legal and liability framework that would apply if it operates a ski resort through it’s Mountain Recreation Enterprise. While operating a ski area carries responsibilities, the Town’s liability exposure as a municipality is different from that of a private ski operator.


Under the Colorado Governmental Immunity Act (CGIA), municipalities are generally immune from lawsuits, except in certain limited situations defined by law. One such situation involves injuries caused by a dangerous condition of a public recreation facility that the Town knew or should have known about and failed to properly maintain.


Even when immunity is waived, the CGIA places statutory limits on damages that may be recovered. These limits help provide predictability and reduce financial exposure compared to private ski operators, who may face higher or unlimited damages under different legal standards.


The Town’s approach combines this legal framework with established risk management, safety, and maintenance practices to operate the resort responsibly as a public recreation asset.


40. What background checks or child safety steps will ski school follow?


The Town through its Mountain Recreation Enterprise intends to follow applicable laws and industry-standard practices related to background checks and child safety for ski school operations. The Town also expects to continue appropriate child safety protocols that are currently in place at Eldora Mountain Resort, subject to review and alignment with Town policies.


Specific procedures and requirements will be finalized as part of the transition and will be communicated once they are established.

No decisions have been made regarding immediate repairs to, or replacement of, the Corona lift. The Town has been performing extensive diligence of the current assets and operations of Eldora.


Following the acquisition, the Town expects to engage in broader planning to evaluate capital improvement needs and long-term priorities across the mountain. Any future decisions about lift repairs, upgrades, or replacement would be considered through that process and would be subject to funding availability and required approvals.


The Town’s due diligence report identifies several potential future capital expansion projects across the mountain, including a mountain-top restaurant, lift infrastructure such as the Corona lift, and the Jolly Jug expansion. These items are for planning purposes only and do not represent approved projects, timelines, or funding commitments.


Separately, the bond documents are anticipated to include a mechanism for setting aside a portion of available revenues for capital improvements, including lift-related needs.


VI.· Permits, Land & Environment


42. What is the status of the Forest Service Special Use Permit and the process for its transfer?


The Town is currently working with the U.S. Forest Service regarding the Special Use Permit required to operate the ski area and use U.S. Forest Service land. The permit is a required approval for the transaction to move forward.


Under the Asset Purchase Agreement, closing is conditioned on the Town obtaining a new Special Use Permit from the U.S. Forest Service that is substantially similar to the existing permit for Eldora Mountain Resort. The transaction cannot be completed unless this condition and other customary closing conditions are satisfied.


The timing and outcome of the permit process are subject to the U.S. Forest Service’s review and approval process.


43. Did we buy water rights, or will we pay extra for snowmaking water?


Eldora’s operations currently rely on a combination of owned and leased water rights that have supported snowmaking and resort operations under typical conditions. Under the Asset Purchase Agreement, the Town will acquire the owned water rights and related water infrastructure used in resort operations as part of the purchase.


Some water used by the resort has historically come from supplemental water leases. These leases are expected to transfer where allowed, but future renewals cannot be guaranteed and may depend on third parties and regulatory requirements.


Future water availability and costs will depend on operating conditions and applicable legal requirements.


44. [NEW] Water is another question. Eldora might have the water rights to make snow now but will it have the water rights to make snow in 10 years or will we need more water rights?


Water rights and supply are an important operational consideration. Eldora’s snowmaking and resort operations rely on a combination of owned water rights and supplemental leased supplies.


Under the Asset Purchase Agreement, the Town would acquire the owned water rights and related infrastructure used in resort operations. Some water has historically come from supplemental annual leases; those arrangements may transfer where permitted, but renewals and future availability depend on third parties, regulatory requirements, and water supply conditions.


Evaluating long-term water needs is part of responsible operational planning. Future needs and potential alternatives would be considered through the post-acquisition master planning process, which will evaluate operational requirements, environmental considerations, and long-term sustainability.


45. [NEW] Is Nederland going to sell its water rights – how would something like that look?


The Town is not currently considering the sale of any water rights associated with the acquisition. The purpose of the acquisition is long-term ownership and stewardship of the resort.


Any future decision to sell, lease, or otherwise transfer water rights or other property interests would require action by the Board of Trustees and would occur through the Town’s public decision-making processes.


At this time, no such proposals are under consideration.


46. [NEW] Will Nederland honor the previous settlement agreements with the US Forest Service signed by Eldora Ski Area/POWDR regarding ski area expansion?


A significant portion of Eldora Mountain Resort operates on U.S. Forest Service land under a federal special use permit. The rights, limitations, and approved improvements associated with that permit remain governed by federal law and the terms of the permit itself.


If the Town becomes the owner, it is anticipated that the Town would obtain a new special use permit consistent with the existing permit framework. The Town expects that the current permit terms and conditions would remain in effect, and nothing would be modified or changed as part of the ownership transition unless approved by the U.S. Forest Service.


47. [NEW] Does Nederland anticipate expansion of the ski area? Are there any plans to renew the 2011-2013 effort?


There are no plans at this time to reopen or revive the broader expansion efforts that were discussed in 2011–2013.


The only expansion currently contemplated is the previously approved Jolly Jug terrain expansion, which is already authorized under the existing U.S. Forest Service special use permit framework. Any work associated with that area would still require compliance with federal permitting requirements and environmental review as applicable.


Beyond that previously approved terrain, there are no additional expansion proposals under consideration.


Any future changes on Forest Service land would remain subject to federal oversight and approval. Longer-term operational planning would occur through future planning processes and public review.


48. [NEW] Are there plans for further land acquisition or development around the ski resort--particularly with regard to Spencer Mountain and the rest of Eldora?


Nothing is currently planned. The Master Planning process will start after the acquisition to think about future impacts. The Town’s current focus has been on acquiring the resort.


49. [NEW] Is Ned willing to limit how much development occurs so the ski area does not become an urban extension of Nederland? Will there be any limits on how much development is allowed at the ski area? Long-term, what prohibits this from being turned into "West Nederland"?


Development at the ski area is constrained by multiple layers of regulation and oversight. These include federal permit requirements, county land use regulations, and—if annexed—Town planning and zoning requirements.


A significant portion of the resort is located on U.S. Forest Service land and is governed by a federal special use permit. That permit framework establishes clear boundaries on terrain, facilities, and environmental impacts. Any expansion or significant change requires federal review and approval.


Local land use regulations and public review processes also govern development on non-federal lands. Together, these regulatory frameworks limit the scale and character of future development.


50. How will expansion be balanced with watershed protection and wildfire mitigation?


No expansion plans have been approved or proposed at this time. Any future consideration of expansion would occur through separate planning, regulatory, and public review processes.


Watershed protection and wildfire mitigation are existing priorities for the Town and would continue to be addressed regardless of the Eldora Mountain Resort acquisition. Any future decisions related to operations or development would be evaluated in light of applicable environmental requirements and community priorities.


51. [NEW] I’m concerned about a wildfire happening up on the mountain. What are the plans?


Wildfire risk is an important concern in forested mountain environments. Eldora currently operates within regional wildfire preparedness and response frameworks that include coordination with local fire protection districts, Boulder County, and state and federal land management agencies.


If the Town becomes the owner, wildfire mitigation and preparedness will be evaluated as part of ongoing operational planning and the post-acquisition master planning process. This includes reviewing vegetation management practices, defensible space, emergency access, and coordination with emergency response agencies.


Wildfire management on U.S. Forest Service lands remains subject to federal oversight and interagency coordination.


52. [NEW] Geography and climate change – we live on the other side of the divide so we are never going to have the snow that Aspen does, and with climate change, we’ve seen the winter we are having right now, so my question is what’s that look like not now or five years but 20 years from now? Do we have that financial modeling and an understanding of what things look like if it can’t be used as a ski resort anymore?


Snowfall variability and climate trends are important considerations for any ski area, particularly on the Front Range. These factors are part of the Town’s evaluation of long-term operational and financial sustainability.


The Town is conducting financial modeling and stress testing with professional advisors to evaluate scenarios, risks, and constraints. Long-horizon assumptions and outputs have not been finalized for public release at this stage, and it would not be appropriate to characterize conclusions before that work is complete and presented through the proper public process.


Mountain resorts across North America adapt to changing conditions through operational strategies such as snowmaking, terrain management, and diversification of year-round recreation. Long-term resilience and adaptation strategies would be evaluated through future planning efforts.


Planning for climate variability and long-term sustainability will be an important topic during the post-acquisition master planning process and broader community discussions.


VII. Annexation


53. What would be the impact on the LEWSD (Lake Eldora Water and Sewer District) if the Town annexes the Eldora Mountain Resort property?


At this time, no changes to LEWSD are proposed or required as part of the acquisition. LEWSD would continue to operate as a separate water and sanitation district unless and until any future action is taken through a separate legal and public process.


The Asset Purchase Agreement includes provisions to preserve existing governance arrangements related to LEWSD during the transition. Specifically, certain resort employees who currently hold small ownership interests connected to LEWSD governance are expected to retain their ability to serve on the LEWSD board, subject to new agreements and continued employment.


Any future changes affecting LEWSD—such as consolidation or restructuring—would require additional approvals, legal steps, and public engagement and are not determined by the acquisition itself.


54. [NEW] Will the Town of Eldora be annexed?


With respect to the residential area of Eldora, Colorado law requires a signed annexation petition from landowners representing more than 50 percent of the landowners and more than 50 percent of the land area before the Town could move forward with annexation. Without that petition, annexation of the residential area could not proceed.


To clarify, annexation of the residential area cannot be initiated by the Town alone; the required petition must come from qualifying landowners.


55. [NEW] Why would there be no vote on annexation? I thought the NMC calls for a public vote for property larger than 10 acres?


Regarding the ski resort property, once the acquisition is complete, the Town may consider whether to pursue annexation of Town-owned land. Under the Colorado Municipal Annexation Act, a private landowner petition is not required when a municipality annexes property it owns, and the Town would act as the petitioner.


Any annexation must follow the requirements of the Colorado Municipal Annexation Act as well as the Nederland Municipal Code, including eligibility under state law and all required notice and public hearing procedures.


While the Nederland Municipal Code includes provisions that may require an election in certain annexation circumstances, those provisions do not apply to annexation of Town-owned property. Any such consideration would still follow the required public notice and hearing process.


Any future annexation proposal would be considered through the Town’s public processes and in compliance with applicable law.


56. [NEW] If Nederland annexes the ski area will there be any limits placed on annexing other lands adjacent to the path of annexation?


Annexing the ski area would not create automatic authority to annex other properties along that corridor.


Any additional property would need to independently qualify for annexation under the Colorado Municipal Annexation Act and Chapter 15 of the Nederland Municipal Code. That includes, in part, meeting statutory contiguity requirements and completing public notice and hearing requirements. In many cases under Nederland’s code, annexation also requires voter approval unless a specific exemption applies.


In practical terms, the Town cannot simply annex neighboring properties because they lie along an annexation route. Annexation is typically initiated by property owners through a petition process. If property owners along that corridor wish to pursue annexation in the future, they could petition, but that decision would be theirs and subject to the same legal and public review requirements.


An annexation boundary does not function as a pipeline for automatic expansion; each annexation must stand on its own legally and procedurally.


57. [NEW] If Ned annexed the ski area, are they willing to prohibit annexation of intervening private lands between the town and the ski area?


Annexation decisions are legislative actions made by the Board of Trustees under state law and the Municipal Code. A current Board cannot bind future Boards or preemptively prohibit all future annexations.


However, annexing the ski area would not automatically annex or authorize annexation of intervening private lands.


In practical terms, intervening properties cannot be annexed simply because they lie between the Town and the ski area. Each potential annexation must be evaluated separately through the legal and public review process.


58. [NEW] What planning and zoning control will Nederland have regarding the property that current 3rd-party ownership does not have?


Planning and zoning authority depends on whether the property is inside or outside Town limits.


If the ski area remains unincorporated, land use and development approvals fall under Boulder County’s land use regulations and review processes.


If the property is annexed into Nederland, it would become subject to the Town’s Comprehensive Plan, zoning code, and development review procedures. That means zoning designations and local land use approvals would be determined as required by the Town’s regulations rather than the County regulations and review process.


A significant portion of the ski area operates on U.S. Forest Service land under a federal special use permit. Federal lands are generally not subject to municipal annexation, and it is not anticipated that these areas would be annexed. In any event, federal jurisdiction would remain in place, and any improvements or operational changes on Forest Service land would continue to require federal oversight, environmental review, and permitting.


Ownership and zoning authority are separate issues. Property inside Town limits is subject to Town regulations; property outside Town limits remains under County jurisdiction unless annexed; and federal lands remain under federal control.


VIII. · Infrastructure & Public Services


59. Who fixes Shelf Road, and how does that tie into Boulder County’s maintenance deal?


Shelf Road is currently a Boulder County road and remains under the County’s jurisdiction. The Town’s acquisition of Eldora Mountain Resort does not change the ownership or maintenance responsibility for Shelf Road.


The Town recognizes the importance of Shelf Road as an access route and will continue to coordinate and communicate with Boulder County regarding its condition and maintenance. If the Eldora property were to be annexed into the Town in the future, road maintenance responsibilities would be evaluated at that time through a separate legal and public process.


60. Will police and fire service needs at Eldora strain Town services?


At this time, the Town does not expect Eldora’s operations to place undue strain on police or fire services. Eldora currently receives law enforcement support through a contract with the Boulder County Sheriff’s Office for extra-duty officers, in addition to regional fire protection services that also serve the Town of Nederland. The acquisition does not change these existing service arrangements.


Any additional service needs related to Eldora’s operations would be evaluated as part of ongoing operations. The Town expects that costs associated with operating the resort, including contracted public safety services, would be addressed through revenues generated by Eldora, consistent with its operation as a Town enterprise. Any future changes would be reviewed in coordination with service providers and in compliance with applicable requirements.


61. [NEW] How can you run a ski area and not have sidewalks?


Sidewalks and pedestrian safety are important community priorities. Work is underway to improve pedestrian connections downtown and along key corridors.


The Town is applying for grant funding again this year to begin design of additional downtown sidewalks. The Downtown Development Authority is also advancing related improvements. This summer, the TIPS project is expected to add new sidewalk improvements along First Street west of the highway, and the SMS project includes additional sidewalk connections near the library along with new crosswalks.


The proposed purchase of Eldora Mountain Resort is structured differently. The financing would come from the Mountain Recreation Enterprise Fund and be supported by ski area revenues, not from the Town’s general fund, or the capital dollars typically used for streets and sidewalks.


Because of this structure, funding associated with the resort cannot simply be redirected to sidewalk construction.


The purpose of the acquisition is long-term community resilience. Eldora influences employment, local business activity, housing demand, and regional economic stability. The enterprise structure is designed so the mountain supports itself financially while strengthening the broader local economy.


Sidewalk and street improvements will continue to be planned and funded through transportation, capital, and grant programs. These efforts address different community needs and are pursued through different funding tools.


The Town will not be overseeing the day-to-day operations of the ski resort. The existing on-mountain team will continue running operations, and POWDR will remain involved in a support role for at least the first two years.


62. [NEW] On land use and infrastructure – what conversations are happening about traffic flow, parking, and transportation to and from the mountain? Is there conversation to support a small mountain hub to support local businesses rather than push growth outward?


No decisions have been made. Following acquisition, the Town anticipates evaluating long-term priorities, constraints, and opportunities through a broader Master Planning process. Any specific initiatives would require separate public processes, coordination with land managers and agencies, and identification of funding and operational.


IX. · Community Benefits & Local Access


63. What happens to Eldora profits once the debt is paid down?


The bond documents establish the order in which Eldora’s revenues are applied while the bonds are outstanding, including the payment of debt service, required reserves, and other obligations. So long as the bonds remain outstanding, revenues must be used in accordance with those requirements.


After all bond obligations have been satisfied or the bonds are no longer outstanding, the use of Eldora’s revenues would be governed by applicable law and Town policy in effect at that time. No decisions have been made regarding the use of revenues after the bonds are repaid. Additional bonds may be issued in the future and are also allowable to furnish enhancements to Eldora.


Any future determinations regarding the use of Eldora revenues would be subject to future policy decisions, operational needs, and legal considerations, and cannot be determined at this time.


64. Could the Nederhaus turn into Prop 123 friendly housing?


At this time, no decisions or plans have been made to convert Nederhaus into affordable housing under Proposition 123 or any other housing program. Proposition 123 is a voter-approved program intended to support affordable housing, but any potential use of Nederhaus for housing would require separate evaluation, approvals, funding decisions, and public processes.


Any future consideration of housing use would depend on factors such as property conditions, legal requirements, funding availability, and community priorities. The acquisition of Eldora does not authorize or require any change in the use of Nederhaus.


65. [NEW] The workforce and housing and smart growth – if the ski area succeeds then the demand needs for employees and services will also grow, is the town open to working with responsible developers who understand mountain design and can help deliver housing that fits Nederland’s characteristics while supporting the local economy?


Future housing and development proposals would be evaluated through the Town’s planning and review processes. The 2025 Comprehensive Plan identifies community priorities and design considerations intended to maintain Nederland’s character while addressing housing and economic needs.


Any specific projects or partnerships would require separate review, public input, and approvals.


X. · Year-Round & Future Opportunities


66. What is the town's timeline for the proposed summer activities?


No timeline has been established for summer activities. Any evaluation of potential summer operations would occur after the Town acquires the mountain and would be part of a separate planning process.


That evaluation would include a review of applicable Boulder County permits, the U.S. Forest Service Special Use Permit, and the operational, environmental, and financial considerations associated with summer activities. It would also involve coordination and discussions with the Forest Service and other relevant agencies.


No summer programs or expansion plans have been approved at this time, and any future activities would require additional review, approvals, and public engagement before moving forward.


67. Is night skiing on the table?


No decisions have been made regarding night skiing. Any evaluation of night skiing would take place after the Town acquires the mountain and would be part of a separate planning and review process.


That evaluation would include a review of permits, operational costs, logistics, and coordination with the U.S. Forest Service and other relevant agencies. The Town is interested in hearing from the community as part of any future discussion, while recognizing that no plans or approvals are in place at this time.


68. Could we ever build a gondola from Barker Reservoir to the resort?


It’s a fun idea, but right now it’s only that: an idea. No feasibility work is planned.


69. What about Nordic skiing - does it stay?


Absolutely! We know locals particularly love and utilize the Nordic lodge and trails. As a part of the Town’s evaluation of the entirety of Eldora’s operations, we will evaluate Nordic operations once the deal is completed.


70. [NEW] Summertime is 50% of the year and how soon could we expect summertime activities up there? Can we do something small to do something up there right after the sale this summer?


Most new summer activities will require land use review and approvals from the applicable jurisdictions, and in some cases coordination with the U.S. Forest Service if activities occur on federal land. Annexation is one potential pathway for local land use authority but is not the sole determinant of whether activities could occur.


At this stage, no new summer uses have been approved. The timing for any additional activities would depend on regulatory review, operational planning, and applicable permitting processes.


Because these steps take time and require coordination among multiple agencies, it is uncertain the timeline.


71. [NEW] What is your vision for Eldora? How does the Town of Eldora fit into that?


All future planning will operate under the Master Planning process after the acquisition. We currently have no plans for the Town of Eldora.


72. [NEW] Will there be ongoing conversations with the community who want to contribute solutions?


The community participation will be an important part of the new Master Planning process, with opportunities for residents, businesses, and stakeholders to share input through public meetings and other engagement opportunities. That input will help guide future planning priorities.


73. [NEW] You’re talking about the summer time recreation – in order to do this my understanding is that the US Forest Service agreement would need to be substantially amended, what I haven’t heard is if you have been talking to the US Forest Service about what those opportunities look like – how easy is that going to be there are wildlife concerns and attempts by the mountain to expand have been hard to get through.


Certain summer recreation activities may require review or amendment of the U.S. Forest Service special use permit, depending on the scope and location of the activity.


The previously approved Jolly Jug terrain expansion remains authorized under the existing permit framework


The Town’s efforts are focused on maintaining continuity under the existing permit structure. Any future proposals involving additional summer would require evaluation of whether the expanded uses would require Forest Service approval.


It is also important to note that land use approvals on non-federal lands may involve Boulder County or, if annexed, Town land use review processes.


74. [NEW] Will the EMR parking area become available outside of ski-season for the Hessie/Lost Lake traffic, and will Ned be exploring trail access to Hessie/Lost Lake from the parking area?


No decisions have been made. The Town is aware that this topic is of interest to community members and regional partners, including Boulder County. Any potential changes would require coordination with relevant jurisdictions and land managers.


75. [NEW] If the ski area is open year-round after Nederland’s purchase, will all the hiking trails be open again to the public? In particular, will Jenny Creek Trail remain closed per the current rules? Assuming summer operations, how will this impact CR 130 and how will impact be managed?


No decisions have been made. Trail access and closures may involve multiple jurisdictions (including the U.S. Forest Service), and any changes would require coordination and appropriate review and approvals.


The Master Planning process is expected to begin after the acquisition to evaluate future operational considerations and potential impacts. The Town’s current focus is on completing the acquisition.


76. [NEW] Is the town looking at this as simply owning a ski hill or is there a broader vision for housing, tourism, and year-round economic vitality?


What has been decided to date is the ownership and enterprise structure, the use of resort revenues (rather than tax dollars) to support operations and debt service, and the intent to maintain operational continuity through professional resort leadership. Any broader initiatives (housing, major year-round expansions, new development concepts) would require separate decisions, processes, and approvals.


The acquisition has been discussed as one potential element supporting long-term community vitality; however, specific future initiatives have not been adopted.
